# Lunge Biomechanics & Form-Check Research

## 1. Camera View Selection: Side View (45°–90°)

We designed lunge detection for a single webcam placed to the user's side (at a 45°–90° angle), facing either left or right.

**Why a side view instead of a front view:**

* Knee bend, knee-over-toe position, stride length, and torso lean can all be measured directly in 2D pixel coordinates (`pose.px()`), avoiding the guesswork of single-camera depth estimation.
* Single cameras are bad at measuring depth (REHAB24-6 benchmarks show an average error of ~12 cm). Because of this, a side camera cannot reliably detect knees caving inward, and a front camera cannot reliably measure how deeply the knees bend.
* At a strict 90° side angle, the near leg often blocks the camera's view of the far hip, causing MediaPipe to drop it while the near leg and far knee/ankle stay visible. To prevent false `NO_POSE` dropouts, we require at least one full leg (`visibility >= 0.5`), allow lower visibility (`>= 0.3`) for the partially blocked far knee and ankle, and use the near hip's position as a fallback for the hidden far hip.

## 2. Benchmark Datasets & Clinical Literature Review

| Source                                                         | Setup / Focus                               | Key Findings                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Impact on Our Design                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **EC3D** (Zhao et al., 2022)                             | 3D skeleton dataset                         | Labels lunges as `Correct`, `Not low enough`, or `Knee passes toe`.                                                                                                                                                                                        | Established our core checks for lunge depth and knee-past-toe.                                                                                                              |
| **ALEX-GYM-1** (Hassan et al., 2025)                     | 2-camera trainer-annotated dataset          | Tracks 7 cues: `90° knee bend`, `straight back`, `back knee positioning`, `forward gaze`, `shoulder-width heels`, `aligned feet`, and `natural arm movement`. Raw video models beat skeleton-only models by 89–90% on `back knee positioning`. | Confirmed that measuring back-knee floor clearance from skeleton points alone is unreliable; replaced it with back-knee bend angle and the height difference between knees. |
| **REHAB24-6** (Černek et al., 2024) & **UI-PRMD** | Rehab exercise datasets (`Ex5` / `m03`) | Evaluates front knee angle, back knee angle, torso lean, and **foot distance**.                                                                                                                                                                            | Added **stance length (understriding)** as a reliable side-view check that was missing from our initial list.                                                         |
| **Escamilla et al. (2008, *JOSPT*)**                   | Kneecap joint stress during forward lunges  | Short-step lunges (where the knee pushes past the toes) put significantly more stress on the kneecap joint between 60° and 90° of knee bend compared to longer steps.                                                                                          | Justifies flagging both short steps and knees passing toes to keep rehab users safe.                                                                                        |
| **Farrokhi et al. (2008, *JOSPT*)**                    | Effect of torso lean on muscle activation   | A slight forward lean activates the glutes and hamstrings more, while leaning backward adds no lower-body benefit.                                                                                                                                               | Allows a mild forward lean (up to 20°) while flagging excessive forward collapse or backward lean (`<= -12°`).                                                          |

## 3. Error Selection Matrix: Implemented vs. Dropped

### Implemented Checks (5 Form Checks + Squat Rejection)

| Check                                         | Source                                        | How It's Detected (`pose.px()`)                                                                                                                                                                                         | Why We Kept It                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Insufficient Depth**               | Starter (EC3D, ALEX-GYM-1, REHAB24-6)         | Front knee (`<= 105°`) and back knee (`<= 120°`) must bend deep enough. If the user bends to `<= 135°` and stands back up without hitting full depth, the cue stays on screen for 1.5 s (`pose.timestamp_ms`). | Clearly visible from the side; stops half-reps and small stance shifts from counting. |
| **2. Front Knee Past Toes**             | Starter (EC3D) + Escamilla et al.             | Horizontal distance the knee moves past the toe, divided by shin length: `(knee.x - toe.x) / shin_len` (triggers at `>= 0.2`, clears at `<= 0.10`).                                                                 | Works regardless of how far the user is from the camera; protects the kneecap joint.  |
| **3. Torso Lean (Forward & Backward)**  | Refined from starter + Farrokhi et al.        | Angle of the more visible `SHOULDER`–`HIP` line relative to straight vertical (triggers at `>= 20°` forward or `<= -18°` backward, with a clear buffer to prevent flickering).                                 | Easy to measure from the side; avoids penalizing a healthy slight forward lean.       |
| **4. Stance Too Short (Understriding)** | **Added** (REHAB24-6, Escamilla et al.) | Horizontal distance between ankles divided by leg length (triggers at `<= 0.65` during the descent/bottom; clears immediately at `>= 0.72` in any phase).                                                             | Fixes the root cause of knees pushing past toes and cramped depth.                    |
| **5. Forward Head Posture**             | Starter (ALEX-GYM-1 `forward gaze`)         | Angle between the `EAR`–`SHOULDER` line and the torso (triggers at `>= 50°`, clears at `<= 40°`; only runs when ear visibility is `>= 0.6`).                                                                 | Cleanly visible from the side; encourages looking forward for better balance.         |
| **Squat Rejection (Rep Gate)**          | Live testing                                  | At the bottom of the rep, the ankles must be split apart (`>= 0.40` leg length) and one knee must be clearly lower than the other (`>= 0.22` thigh length).                                                           | Stops regular squats (which also hit ~90° knee bend) from registering as lunges.     |

### Dropped Errors (6 Candidate Errors Rejected)

| Error Considered                                | Source                       | Why It Was Dropped for a Single Side Webcam                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Knees Caving In (Dynamic Knee Valgus)** | Clinical physio (FPPA)       | Happens along the depth axis from a side view; single-camera depth estimates aren't accurate enough to catch an ~8° inward cave.                                                                      |
| **Stance Width & Foot Alignment**         | ALEX-GYM-1                   | Side-to-side foot spacing and toe-out angle are hidden in a side profile.                                                                                                                              |
| **Rounded Back (Kyphosis)**               | Starter (`rounded back`)   | MediaPipe only tracks `SHOULDER` and `HIP` endpoints—not the middle of the spine. Replaced with overall torso lean.                                                                               |
| **Back Knee Distance to Floor**           | Starter / ALEX-GYM-1         | Requires calibrating where the floor is and breaks if the camera is tilted. Replaced with back-knee bend angle and knee-height difference.                                                             |
| **Hip Drop (Trendelenburg)**              | Clinical physio              | Requires comparing left and right hip height from the front; the hips overlap in a side view.                                                                                                          |
| **Front Heel Lift & Arm Position**        | Clinical physio / ALEX-GYM-1 | A 1–2 cm heel lift is too small to separate from normal tracking jitter or frame cropping. Rehab patients also often hold a chair or wall for balance, so flagging arm position creates false alarms. |
